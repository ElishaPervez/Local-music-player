Signed-in YouTube access — done

Some songs simply never played before: YouTube would refuse the stream because the app was an anonymous visitor. You can now hand the app your own signed-in YouTube session, and those songs resolve and play like any other.

The short version: a cookie button now lives in the title bar. You paste (or load a file of) your exported YouTube cookies, the app verifies them against YouTube once, and from then on every search and download runs as you instead of as a logged-out stranger. Age-restricted, region-limited, and "sign in to confirm you're not a bot" tracks start working. Two invisible helpers had to be built alongside it so those signed-in streams actually reach the speakers.

1 What you'll see

A cookie button in the title bar. A small dot on it tells you the state at a glance:

Verified — cookies work, YouTube accepted them.
Not verified — imported but not yet checked.
Expired / invalid — the file is stale or malformed.
Rejected — YouTube refused them; time to re-export.

A popover to manage them. Paste the cookies into a masked field (or pick a file), press Ctrl+Enter, and it imports. It shows when they were last updated and last checked, offers a Retry verification button, and a Remove button to wipe them.

The pasted text is cleared after every attempt so it never lingers on screen.

The observable payoff: before this, picking certain tracks ended in "Format error" or a track that resolved but instantly skipped. Now, with verified cookies in place, those same tracks play through.

2 Why one cookie file wasn't enough

Handing yt-dlp your cookies gets it a stream URL — but that URL wouldn't play. Two separate walls sit between "YouTube gave us a link" and "sound comes out", and each needed its own helper. Here's the whole path:

Your cookies
Imported, verified, and stored as a Netscape cookie file the app manages.
→
PO token helper
A tiny local server mints a "proof-of-origin" token to attach to the request.
→
yt-dlp resolves
Signed in and vouched-for, so YouTube returns a real, playable stream URL.
→
Loopback proxy
The player fetches audio from the app itself, not from YouTube directly.
→
Sound plays
Clean audio the webview can't reject.
Wall #1

Signed-in links were being refused (HTTP 403). When the app sent your cookies, YouTube expected a companion "proof-of-origin" token proving the request came from a real browser session. Without it, the resolve appeared to succeed but the stream URL was dead — the track would load and then silently never play.

Fix: a bundled helper (a small Deno server) generates that token on demand. yt-dlp attaches it, and the link comes back alive. If the helper ever fails to start, the app just runs without it — exactly as it did before, no crash.

Wall #2

The player itself rejected the audio. YouTube's audio host can answer the player's first request with a redirect disguised as a web page. The webview treats that as suspicious and blocks the whole media load — the track reports "Format error" and gets skipped.

Fix: a tiny web server runs inside the app on 127.0.0.1. The player asks it for the audio; it fetches from YouTube behind the scenes, follows the redirects, and hands back a clean audio stream the player can't object to.

3 How your cookies are handled

  • They stay on your machine. Cookies are written to the app's own data folder as a standard Netscape cookie file and read only by the local yt-dlp process. Nothing is uploaded anywhere by this feature.
  • The paste field is masked and wiped after each import attempt, so an active session token isn't left sitting on screen. A show/hide toggle lets you check what you pasted.
  • The app tells you the risk plainly: these cookies are a live Google login. The popover warns never to paste them into a chat. Masking only stops casual on-screen exposure — the app still receives the text locally to install it.
  • Verification is a real round-trip. "Verified" means YouTube actually accepted the cookies, not just that the file parsed. Expired or malformed cookies are caught and labelled instead of silently failing later.
  • Concurrent-safe. Two downloads running at once can't stomp on each other's cookie file — writes are serialized, and any session refresh YouTube hands back is merged in rather than overwritten.
