Live wallpapers — done

You can now set a looping video as the background, with the same blur & opacity controls the image background already had. It copies into the app's own storage, and it stops working the moment you can't see it.

The short version: the app already had an image-background feature. I widened it to accept video files. When you pick a .mp4/.webm, it plays as a muted, looping background instead of a still picture. Everything else — importing it into app storage, the blur/opacity sliders, the frosted-glass panels — was reused as-is.

The "Add…" button's file picker now offers video files alongside images. Pick one, and a preview modal shows the clip actually playing while you drag the Blur and Opacity sliders — so you set it to taste before committing.

The background itself

A still image is painted as before. A video plays full-window behind the app, looping silently, cover-cropped to fill any window size. The little thumbnail in Settings shows the video's first frame.

2 "Copies into app storage so it survives deletion"

When you choose a file, the app doesn't point at the original on your disk — it copies the bytes into its own private folder and points at that copy. So if you later move, rename, or delete the file you picked, the wallpaper keeps working.

This is exactly what the image background already did — I reused the same copy step untouched, so video needed no backend change. I did add one thing: when you replace a wallpaper, the old copy is deleted from app storage. Videos can be hundreds of megabytes, so without this, every wallpaper you ever tried would pile up and quietly eat disk space.

3 Keeping it light on the system

A moving video costs more than a still image — that's unavoidable. Here's where the cost actually goes and what stops it from being wasteful.

Biggest win — it pauses when you can't see it.

The video stops decoding entirely the moment the app is minimized or its window is hidden. A paused video does zero work — no CPU, no GPU, no battery drain. When you come back, it resumes from where it left off. So while you're in another app full-screen, the wallpaper is effectively free. This is the single largest saving, and it's the reason a live wallpaper here is reasonable to leave on.

The decode runs on the graphics card, not the CPU.

The app's display engine hands video decoding to your GPU's dedicated video hardware — the same silicon that plays YouTube without spinning up your fans. The clip is also muted, so there's no audio work at all.

The one real cost, and why I left it in.

The app's panels (sidebar, top bar, player) are frosted glass — they blur whatever's behind them. Behind a still image that blur is computed once and cached. Behind a moving video it has to be recomputed every frame, for each panel. That's the main ongoing cost of a live wallpaper.

I kept the frosted look because you asked for "least intensive without compromise" — dropping the glass would be a visible downgrade, and modern graphics cards handle this comfortably. If you ever want the absolute-lowest-power option, the lever is right here: swapping those panels from frosted-glass to solid-tinted when a video is active would remove this per-frame cost entirely. Say the word and I'll add it as a toggle.
