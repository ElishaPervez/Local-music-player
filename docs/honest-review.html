The short version: you've built a genuinely good personal music player with a few tricks the big apps don't bother with. Where it can't compete is the stuff that needs a company behind it — a music catalog you're licensed to use, sound quality guarantees, and your library following you between devices.

🎧  Verdict:  A polished tool for one person on one Windows PC — not a Spotify replacement, and it doesn't need to be.

03Where it genuinely beats the big apps

These aren't participation trophies — each one is something Spotify or YT Music actively won't or can't give you.

↑

You own the files, no subscription

A downloaded playlist plays on a plane, in a dead-zone, or ten years from now after the app is abandoned. Cancel Spotify and your Liked Songs vanish that day — here nothing is rented.

↑

No ads, ever, and it's free

Free Spotify interrupts you with ads and won't let you pick a song on your phone. YouTube shows video ads before songs. Your app has neither — every play is instant and uninterrupted.

↑

Crossfade that actually sounds right

When one song ends the next rises in as the old one fades, using an equal-power blend so the music never dips in volume through the overlap. It even pre-loads the next track a few seconds early, so a streamed song starts the fade on time instead of stuttering. Spotify has crossfade; YouTube Music still doesn't.

↑

The whole of YouTube is your catalog

Live bootlegs, a random SoundCloud-style upload, a 2009 fan remix, regional music the licensed services never cleared — if it's on YouTube, you can play or keep it. Spotify's catalog stops at what labels licensed.

↑

Small touches the big apps skip

Play a song "just once" without wrecking your queue, then the queue picks up exactly where it was. A custom blurred background image. A Discord status showing what you're playing with a live progress bar. Drag-anywhere queue reordering. These are the kind of details a solo builder adds and a committee cuts.

04Where it can't keep up — and why

Being honest: most of these aren't things you forgot to build. They're structural — they need a company, licensing deals, or servers you don't have.

↓

Sound quality is capped by what YouTube hands out ceiling

YouTube serves compressed audio — roughly 128 kbps AAC or Opus. Spotify pushes higher-bitrate streams and Apple Music / Tidal offer lossless. On cheap earbuds nobody hears it; on good headphones a careful listener will notice your tracks sound a touch flatter. You can't fix this — it's whatever YouTube decides to send.

↓

Song info is messy, because it's really video titles data quality

The "artist" is whoever uploaded the video, and the title is the video's title — so you get things like "Song Name (Official Music Video) [4K]" and the artist reading as a random channel. Spotify shows clean artist, album, track number, and real cover art. Yours shows the YouTube thumbnail and the raw title. Fine for playing; ugly for a tidy library.

↓

Your music does not follow you structural

Everything lives on one Windows PC. No phone app, no web player, no syncing your playlists to another machine. Close the laptop and the music stops — there's nothing in your pocket. This is the single biggest gap versus every streaming service, and closing it would mean building servers and accounts.

↓

Discovery is "more like this," not "made for you" shallow

The auto-play radio is smart engineering — it keeps the music going by pulling YouTube's "up next" suggestions, filtering out remixes and songs you already have. But it's reacting to one seed song. Spotify's Discover Weekly and Daylist learn your taste across months and millions of other listeners. Yours can't build a taste profile — it has no idea what you loved last week.

↓

It forgets everything when you close it rough edge

I checked the code: your library, playlists, and history are saved, but the now-playing queue, the volume, and your spot in the song are not. Reopen the app and you're back to a silent 80%-volume blank slate — the song you were halfway through is gone. Every serious player reopens right where you left off. This one's fully in your control to fix.

↓

The keyboard's music buttons don't work rough edge

Pressing the play/pause key on a keyboard or headset does nothing, and the little media popup Windows shows for other players never appears for yours. So you can't pause from another app or the lock screen — you have to click back into the window. Also fixable by you, and it's the kind of thing users quietly expect.

↓

Standing on legally shaky ground risk

Downloading audio from YouTube breaks YouTube's terms of service, and the tool it relies on gets blocked by YouTube from time to time — when that happens, search and playback simply stop working until the tool is updated. For personal use it's a grey area many people live in; but it means this can never become a real product you sell or list in an app store, and it can break out from under you on any given week.

↓

No lyrics, no podcasts, no sharing, no social out of scope

Not criticisms so much as scope: the streaming services are entertainment platforms with lyrics scrolling in time, podcasts, shareable links, and collaborative playlists. Yours is a focused player. That focus is a feature — just know that's the trade.

06Who it's actually for

💚 Perfect for

Someone at a desk who wants a free, ad-free, offline player for music that's already on YouTube — and likes owning the files rather than renting them.

😐 Fine, with caveats

A tinkerer who doesn't mind messy song titles and the occasional week where YouTube blocks the downloader until it's updated.

❌ Wrong tool for

Anyone who listens on their phone, wants lossless audio and clean metadata, or expects the app to learn their taste over time.

07The three fixes that would move the needle most

Ranked by how much a real user would feel it, versus how much work it is. All three are yours to do — no company or licensing required.

1. Remember where you left off high impact · low effort

Save the queue, volume, and playback position, and restore them on launch. This is the difference between "a demo I reopen from scratch" and "my music player." You already persist everything else — it's the same mechanism applied to the player state.

2. Wire up the media keys high impact · low effort

Make the keyboard/headset play-pause button work and show the standard Windows media popup. Users don't praise this when it works, but they immediately distrust a music app where it's missing.

3. Clean up the song titles medium impact · medium effort

Strip the "(Official Video) [4K Remaster]" noise from titles and take a real guess at artist vs. song. Turns a scruffy list of video names into something that reads like an actual library.

Be honest about the ceiling context

Audio quality, real personalized recommendations, and cross-device sync are not weekend fixes — they need YouTube to serve better audio (it won't), a recommendation engine trained on lots of listeners, and servers. It's fine to simply not compete there.

Bottom line: As a piece of software this punches well above its weight — the playback engine, crossfade, and auto-radio are more carefully built than a lot of shipped apps. Its limits are almost entirely the ones a solo project on one PC can't escape: no licensed catalog, no servers, no phone. Judged as "a personal music player I built," it's a clear success. Judged as "a Spotify competitor," it was never going to be one — and trying to make it one is the wrong goal.
